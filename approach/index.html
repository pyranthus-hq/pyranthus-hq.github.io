Our approach

The workflow is the starting point.

A useful system needs more than a capable model. It needs the right context, the right tools and a clear division of responsibility.

01

Understand the work

Map the actual job, including the exceptions. Identify who owns the outcome and what a better result would mean.

A shared baseline: time, quality, cost or throughput.

02

Build a bounded slice

Connect a small set of approved systems. Give the agent focused tools and define where it must stop for a person.

A working path with explicit permissions and failure handling.

03

Evaluate the behaviour

Test realistic tasks, bad inputs, missing information and unavailable tools. Review evidence with the people doing the work.

An acceptance decision based on task performance, not a polished demo.

04

Make it operable

Document the workflow, agree escalation and rollback, and enable the team to use and improve it. Expand only where the evidence supports it.

An accountable owner, an operating guide and a measured next step.

Make the consequential
decisions explicit.

Build or buy?

Use existing software when it fits. Custom work should earn its cost through a specific business need.

Agent or deterministic code?

Use models where interpretation helps. Keep calculations, permissions and critical business rules in code.

Autonomy or approval?

Agree action boundaries up front. High-impact changes need an accountable review path.

Pilot or scale?

A successful demonstration is a reason to evaluate further. Production requires operational evidence.

Start with one repeated job.

Tell us what the team does, where the work gets stuck, and what you want to change.
